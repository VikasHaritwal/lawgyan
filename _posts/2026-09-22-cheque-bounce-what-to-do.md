A returned cheque is one of the most common money disputes in India, and the law gives you a strong remedy. The catch is that **the remedy runs on strict deadlines**. Miss one and you may lose the criminal route entirely.

## When does Section 138 apply?

Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 makes it an offence when a cheque is dishonoured because:

- there wasn't enough money in the account, or
- the amount exceeded the arrangement the drawer had with the bank.

The cheque must have been given for a **legally enforceable debt or liability**. A cheque given as a gift, for example, won't qualify. Once the cheque and signature are admitted, the law presumes it was given for a debt (Section 139). It's then for the drawer to prove otherwise.

## The timeline at a glance

| Step | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Present the cheque to your bank | Within **3 months** of the date on the cheque |
| Send a written demand notice to the drawer | Within **30 days** of the bank telling you it bounced |
| Drawer may pay | Within **15 days** of receiving your notice |
| File the complaint in court | Within **1 month** after those 15 days end |

## Step by step

### 1. Keep the return memo

Your bank will return the cheque with a memo stating the reason for dishonour. Keep the original cheque and the memo safe. They are your primary evidence.

### 2. Send a legal notice within 30 days

The notice must demand payment of the cheque amount. Send it by registered post with acknowledgement due (and, ideally, by courier and email too), and keep every receipt and tracking record.

### 3. Wait 15 days

If the drawer pays within 15 days of receiving the notice, the matter ends there. If they don't, the offence is complete.

### 4. File the complaint within a month

The complaint is filed before the magistrate having jurisdiction where **your** bank branch (where you deposited the cheque) is located. If you miss this window, the court can condone the delay, but only if you show sufficient cause. Don't rely on that.

## What can the court do?

The drawer can be punished with imprisonment of up to **two years**, a fine of up to **twice the cheque amount**, or both. The court can also order the drawer to pay **interim compensation of up to 20%** of the cheque amount while the case is pending (Section 143A).

The offence is compoundable, which means the parties can settle at any stage, and many cases do.

## Can I also file a civil suit?

Yes. The criminal complaint and a civil suit for recovery are separate remedies and can run side by side. A summary suit under Order XXXVII of the Code of Civil Procedure is often used for this.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Original cheque and bank return memo
- [ ] Copy of the demand notice
- [ ] Postal receipts and delivery proof
- [ ] Evidence of the underlying debt (agreement, invoices, messages, bank transfers)
- [ ] A calendar with every deadline marked

> **The short version:** act within 30 days of the bounce, keep every document, and speak to an advocate early. The deadlines are where most of these cases are won or lost.
